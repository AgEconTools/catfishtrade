About the data

What's counted, how it's measured, and where it comes from.

What's counted

All U.S. trade in catfish (the order Siluriformes). From 2012 on, the Harmonized System codes that hold only catfish:

CodeProductCounted in
0304.62Frozen filletsImports; exports (as fresh, see below)
0304.32Fresh or chilled filletsImports and exports
0303.24Whole or dressed, frozenImports and exports
0302.72Whole or dressed, fresh or chilledImports and exports
0304.51.0010, .0015Other meat, fresh or chilled (Ictalurus and Pangasius only)Imports

Mixed codes that also hold other fish (such as tilapia, carp or eels) are left out, because they can't be split.

2005 to 2011

These years come from NOAA Fisheries' Foreign Trade database, which carries the Census Bureau's own trade figures under the catfish codes in use before 2012 (they match Census to the pound where both exist). Imports cover the same species and products as later years, except a few other-catfish whole fish. Exports before 2012 count fillets only; whole and dressed catfish exports are counted from 2012, so export totals step up that year.

Why it starts in 2005

Catfish imports were first reported by species in July 2004. Before that, much of the basa and tra imported from Vietnam was recorded as "marine fish" or "freshwater fish" fillets, mixed with other fish, so earlier catfish import figures would undercount it. 2005 is the first full year with complete catfish detail.

Species

Import records name the species: channel catfish (Ictalurus), Pangasius (basa and tra), or other catfish (Clarias, Silurus and others). Export records don't name a species.

Fresh and frozen exports

Exports are split into fresh and frozen the way NOAA Fisheries names them: frozen fillets (0304.62) count as fresh along with fresh fillets and fresh whole fish, and only frozen whole or dressed fish (0303.24) counts as frozen. Census's own descriptions would put frozen fillets under frozen.

How it's measured

  • Imports are imports for consumption (pounds) and the customs value of general imports (dollars), the basis NOAA Fisheries uses. A few shipments show a value but no pounds; they count in dollars only.
  • Exports are domestic exports only: catfish of U.S. origin. Re-exports of imported catfish are left out.
  • Pounds are product weight, as shipped, converted from Census's kilograms (1 kg = 2.20462 lb). A pound of fillets is a pound of fillets, not the live weight of the fish it came from.
  • Dollars are the declared customs value. Price per pound is dollars divided by pounds: an average unit value, not a market price. Prices resting on under 10,000 lb are marked few lb.
  • Inflation. Dollars are shown as reported unless you switch to adjusted dollars on the Imports and Exports tabs. Adjusted dollars are converted to the newest month's dollars with the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U, U.S. city average, U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics). BLS published no CPI for October 2025 because of the government shutdown; that month uses the midpoint of September and November 2025.
  • Duties are not shown. Census's duty figure leaves out antidumping and countervailing duties, so it understates what importers pay.

When it updates

The U.S. Census Bureau releases each month's trade about five weeks after the month ends, at 8:30 a.m. Eastern on dates it publishes about a year ahead. This page updates the morning of each release. Each update also re-pulls the current and previous year, so recent months pick up Census's revisions.
