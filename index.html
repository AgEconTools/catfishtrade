About the data

What's counted, how it's measured, and where it comes from.

What's counted

All U.S. trade in catfish, of every species. By U.S. law, only North American catfish (channel, blue and their relatives) can be sold as "catfish" in the United States, but trade codes count all catfish species, including Pangasius (basa, tra, swai), so this page does too. Use the Species filter on the Imports tab to see Ictalurus alone.

The trade codes counted from 2012 on, each holding only catfish:

Mixed codes that also hold other fish (such as tilapia, carp or eels) are left out, because they can't be split. NOAA Fisheries counts some of those codes (mostly dried, salted or smoked fillets) in its catfish totals, so its import totals run about 1.5% higher than this page's in pounds and 2% higher in dollars.

2005 to 2011

These years come from NOAA Fisheries' Foreign Trade database, which carries the Census Bureau's own trade figures under the catfish codes in use before 2012 (they match Census's own figures where both exist). Imports cover the same species and products as later years, apart from a small amount of whole fish of other catfish species. Exports before 2012 count fillets only, not whole or dressed fish, and aren't comparable with later years. The step up in 2012 isn't only whole fish: frozen fillet exports also jump under the new codes.

Why it starts in 2005

Catfish imports were first reported by species in July 2004. Before that, much of the basa and tra imported from Vietnam was recorded as "marine fish" or "freshwater fish" fillets, mixed with other fish, so earlier catfish import figures would undercount it. 2005 is the first full year with complete catfish detail.

Species

Import records name the species: Ictalurus (channel and blue catfish), Pangasius (basa, tra and swai), or other catfish (other species, or none named). Export records don't name a species. In 2018 to 2020, a large share of Vietnam's frozen fillets (about a third in 2019) was entered under the code for other catfish fillets (0304.62.0030) instead of the Pangasius code, so other catfish rises and Pangasius falls in those years. Vietnam's catfish is almost all Pangasius, so this most likely reflects how shipments were declared, not a change in species. Since 2024, most of China's whole frozen catfish has been entered as other catfish rather than Ictalurus, so the Ictalurus filter may understate China. Since April 2026, China has also entered some fillets as fresh or chilled.

Export products

Exports are shown as four products, by trade code: fresh fillets, frozen fillets, whole fresh fish and whole frozen fish. NOAA Fisheries' product names group frozen fillets (0304.62) with fresh, so NOAA's "fresh" catfish exports equal fresh fillets, frozen fillets and whole fresh fish here.

How it's measured

  • Imports. Pounds count fish that cleared customs for use in the United States (Census calls these imports for consumption). Dollars are the customs value of all arrivals (general imports). NOAA Fisheries reports catfish trade the same way. A few shipments arrived but had not cleared customs for use in the United States that month, so they show a value but no pounds; they count in dollars only.
  • Exports are domestic exports only: catfish of U.S. origin. Re-exports of imported catfish are left out.
  • Pounds are product weight as shipped (not live weight), converted from Census's kilograms (1 kg = 2.20462 lb).
  • Dollars for imports are customs value: the price paid for the fish, not counting freight and insurance to the United States or duties. For exports, dollars are the value at the U.S. port (free alongside ship), including inland freight but not ocean shipping. Price per pound is dollars divided by pounds. It is the average declared value, not a market price. Prices based on under 10,000 lb are tagged under 10K lb, and a country's change isn't shown when either period is under 10,000 lb (its details give the earlier period's pounds instead). Price charts leave out years under 1,000 lb.
  • Inflation. Dollars are shown as reported unless you switch to adjusted dollars on the Imports and Exports tabs. Adjusted dollars are converted to the newest month's dollars with the Consumer Price Index (CPI-U, Bureau of Labor Statistics). BLS published no CPI for October 2025 because of the government shutdown; that month uses the midpoint of September and November 2025.
  • Duties are not shown. Census's duty figure leaves out antidumping and countervailing duties, so it understates what importers pay.

When it updates

The U.S. Census Bureau releases each month's trade about five weeks after the month ends, on a schedule it publishes about a year ahead. This page updates the morning of each release. Each update also refreshes the current and previous year, so recent months pick up Census's revisions.
